Monumental Structures and Everyday Artifacts

Archaeological excavations in the City of David have uncovered a wealth of artifacts that illuminate Jerusalem’s ancient past and confirm its historical significance. Among the most notable finds are monumental fortifications and the remains of a large stepped stone structure, which some scholars associate with royal or administrative functions from the time of the biblical monarchy. Pottery shards, storage jars, and household implements dating from the Bronze Age to the Babylonian destruction in 586 BCE reveal the daily life of the city’s inhabitants, offering insight into their diet, trade networks, and domestic activities.

The Siloam Inscription and Biblical Connections

One of the most famous discoveries is the Siloam Inscription, a First Temple period Hebrew text carved into the wall of Hezekiah’s Tunnel. This inscription records the completion of the tunnel, which channeled water from the Gihon Spring into the city, reflecting advanced engineering skills of the time and providing physical evidence for biblical accounts. Additional finds, such as clay seal impressions (bullae) bearing names of officials mentioned in the Bible, further strengthen the connection between the City of David and the historical figures who once governed there.

Luxury Goods, Trade, and Destruction Layers

Other remarkable artifacts include fragments of luxury goods, such as ivory inlays, jewelry, and imported ceramics, which speak to Jerusalem’s wealth and its role as a hub of international trade during certain periods. Excavators have also unearthed evidence of destruction layers—charred remains, collapsed walls, and arrowheads—that align with historical records of conquests by the Babylonians and other ancient powers. Together, these archaeological discoveries not only enrich our understanding of the City of David’s political and cultural history but also serve as tangible links between the biblical narrative and the physical remains preserved beneath modern Jerusalem.
