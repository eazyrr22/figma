figures

king david

The City of David is closely tied to the figure of King David, who, according to the biblical narrative, captured the Jebusite stronghold and established it as the capital of the united Kingdom of Israel in the 10th century BCE. David’s decision to move the political and spiritual center to Jerusalem was pivotal, as it provided a neutral site between the northern and southern tribes and secured the city’s religious significance for generations. His reign in the City of David is remembered not only for military victories and territorial expansion but also for laying the foundations for the First Temple, which his son would later build.

king solomon

King Solomon, David’s son and successor, is another central figure connected to the City of David. While the city served as his administrative and royal seat, Solomon’s most enduring contribution was the construction of the First Temple on the nearby Temple Mount, turning Jerusalem into the spiritual heart of the Israelite nation. The biblical and historical traditions also link him to the city’s prosperity, monumental architecture, and expansion beyond the original ridge, marking a period of cultural and political flourishing.

King Hezekiah

Other significant figures associated with the City of David include King Hezekiah of Judah, who reigned in the late 8th century BCE. Hezekiah is credited with fortifying the city against Assyrian invasion, most famously by commissioning the construction of the Siloam Tunnel (or Hezekiah’s Tunnel) to secure Jerusalem’s water supply from the Gihon Spring. His defensive measures and religious reforms are documented both in the Hebrew Bible and in archaeological findings within the City of David, leaving a tangible legacy that connects ancient leadership with the city’s physical remains.
